01 / about

Data person by training, finance person by curiosity.

I got into data science through code and stayed for the questions money raises. Can you see a recession coming before the headlines do? How should a portfolio change when the economy starts to wobble? Why did a model say no to a loan applicant? Those are the problems I like spending weekends on.

My approach is simple: start with a clear question, get the data right, and be strict about testing. That means no peeking at future data, comparing against simple baselines, and explaining the result in plain language. A model that only looks good in a notebook isn't worth much.

Away from the screen, I've handled real money too, running the finances for VIT's Riviera and Gravitas festivals, where a careful spreadsheet saved events more than 40% of their budgets.

portfolio · RLunder review

Teaching an AI to rebalance a portfolio

📄 Paper submitted to Financial Innovation (Springer)
"If an index can sense stress, can a trading agent use it to protect a portfolio?"

I extended the stress index into an adaptive version and gave it to a reinforcement-learning agent that decides how to split money across six ETFs: equities, bonds, gold and real estate.

6assets
5strategies compared
0.56Sharpe (vs 0.67 Markowitz)
How I built it
  • Index weights are re-learned over time with rolling ridge regression, and only updated when the model is confident.
  • A PPO agent sees macro, market and stress signals together and outputs portfolio weights.
  • Benchmarked against equal-weight and Markowitz portfolios, and against RL without the index, using only past data at every step.
  • Honest result so far: on 2022–2024 test data, Markowitz still had the best Sharpe. A longer training run and more test periods are next.

risk · explainable AI2025

CreditLens: a credit model that explains itself

"If a bank rejects you, shouldn't it be able to say why — and be fair about it?"

A credit-default model that predicts risk and shows the reasons behind each decision, then checks whether those reasons are fair across groups and stable when the input changes slightly.

0.81ROC-AUC
85%bad borrowers caught
−47%total cost
How I built it
  • Trained on the German Credit (UCI) dataset; compared logistic regression, XGBoost and random forest, and picked random forest.
  • Used a cost-based cut-off (0.27) instead of 0.5, so the model catches 85% of bad borrowers instead of 35%.
  • SHAP shows which features push each applicant's risk up or down.
  • Fairness checks across demographic groups, plus stability tests on the explanations themselves.
  • Wrapped it all in a Streamlit dashboard: probability, risk band, reasons and diagnostics.

macro · stress testing2025 — now

A digital twin of the economy

"What happens to markets and credit if rates spike, or oil doubles, or both?"

A simulator built on 26 years of real macro and market data that lets you throw shocks at the economy and watch the knock-on effects on markets, credit risk and recovery time.

26 yrsof data
10crisis scenarios
~½won by the RL policy
How I built it
  • Pulled GDP, inflation, rates, unemployment, FX, equities and bond yields from FRED, the World Bank and the IMF.
  • Classified each period as expansion, slowdown, recession or recovery.
  • Trained a PPO policy to respond to shocks and compared it with doing nothing, a fixed policy and rule-based responses across 10 crisis scenarios. It won about half, so it helps in some crises but not all.
